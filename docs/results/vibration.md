# Motor vibration and gyro filters (ArduCopter 4.7.3 SITL)

SITL shakes the gyro at each rotor's spin frequency (`SIM_VIB_MOT_MAX` = 350, `SIM_GYR1_RND` = 20, as ArduPilot's own notch test). The batch sampler logged the raw gyro at 1 kHz before and after the filters; DroneLab's log doctor found the peak with an FFT and recommended the notch. Generated by `python -m backend.sitl.vibration --write`.

**Motor noise found:** 192 Hz, 52 dB above the noise floor (before the filters). **Recommended notch:** `INS_HNTCH_ENABLE` = 1, `INS_HNTCH_MODE` = 1, `INS_HNTCH_FREQ` = 192, `INS_HNTCH_BW` = 96, `INS_HNTCH_ATT` = 40, `INS_HNTCH_HMNCS` = 1, `INS_HNTCH_REF` = 0.37.

| Filters | Noise left at the motor frequency (after filters) | Gyro noise above 30 Hz (RMS, after filters) | Gyro delay at 10 Hz (theory) | Attitude error in a 10 m dash (RMS / max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-pass 20 Hz (default) | 0.28 deg/s | 0.018 deg/s | 12.0 ms (43°) | 1.07° / 3.7° |
| Low-pass 80 Hz | 4.34 deg/s | 0.279 deg/s | 2.8 ms (10°) | 1.08° / 3.7° |
| Low-pass 80 Hz + harmonic notch | 0.12 deg/s | 0.028 deg/s | 3.1 ms (11°) | 1.10° / 3.8° |

- *Delay* is the phase lag of the low-pass (and notch) at 10 Hz, computed from the filter equations: the controller sees the drone's rotation that much late. Less delay = the rate PIDs can be tuned sharper.
- **Attitude tracking was the same with all three** (1.07–1.10° RMS): ArduPilot's default rate PIDs are gentle enough to work behind 12 ms of delay. The delay you save is room to tune them sharper (AUTOTUNE does that); we didn't retune here, so it doesn't show yet.
- The 20 Hz default is safe on almost any drone, but it costs about four times the delay. A notch lets you raise the low-pass: it removes only a narrow band around the motor frequency (and follows it with the throttle, `INS_HNTCH_MODE` = 1).
- On a real drone the peak comes from your props and motors; balance props and soft-mount the flight controller first, then measure, then notch.
